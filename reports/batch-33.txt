1. WHAT I DID
-------------

PART 1 — Product photos break; reordering was rough on the phone and
impossible on PC

  THE REAL CAUSES OF THE BROKEN PHOTOS (there were two):

  a) The photo editor threw away every preview each time the list changed.
     A photo you just picked from the gallery is shown from a temporary
     in-browser link (a "blob:" link). The old code deleted ALL of these
     links and made new ones whenever anything in the list changed: adding
     a photo, removing one, and every reorder. For a moment the thumbnails
     pointed at links that no longer existed. On a phone with big camera
     photos, Chrome is often still loading a photo when that happens, or
     has to load it again after you come back from the gallery app. The
     photo then fails, and you see the white box with "Product image 1".
     Now each thumbnail keeps its own link for as long as it is on screen.
     The link is released only when you remove that photo or leave the
     editor (after saving).

  b) The app's offline photo store (the "service worker") could keep a
     broken answer for 30 days. When the browser asks for a photo, the
     worker could not see whether the answer was a real photo or an error.
     Supabase answers "400" for a missing file. So a missing file, or a
     passing hiccup on a slow connection, was stored as if it were the
     photo and shown broken again and again. I checked that Supabase allows
     the safer way of asking (CORS) for our photos. The worker now asks
     that way, sees the real answer, and keeps only real photos. It uses a
     new store name, and the app deletes the old store when it starts, so
     nothing stored the old way is ever shown again.

  Also: saved photos in the editor now show the small copy first (lighter
  on the phone), then the full photo. If neither loads, you see a calm
  grey box with a Retry button, never the browser's broken-image icon.

  REORDERING, rebuilt with one standard library (dnd-kit, widely used and
  well maintained):
    - Phone: hold a photo for about 0.2 seconds, then drag. A quick swipe
      still scrolls the row, so scrolling and dragging don't fight.
    - PC: just drag with the mouse. (It was impossible before, partly
      because the browser started its own "drag the picture" action.
      That is now switched off for these thumbnails.)
    - Keyboard: Tab to a photo, Space, arrow keys, Space.
    - The other photos slide aside smoothly, with no gaps or overlaps. The
      row scrolls by itself when you drag near its edge.
    - The first photo has a small "Main" label (it used to say "Cover",
      on top of the drag handle). A short hint under the row says "Hold
      and drag a photo to reorder. The first one is the main photo."
    - Dragging photo files in from the PC desktop still works as before.
    - Saving keeps the new order exactly, and each small copy stays with
      its own photo (checked by the tests).
  This photo editor is the only one with several photos. Option (variant)
  photos use a separate one-photo picker that has no reordering, so it
  didn't need this change.

PART 2 — Square box behind rounded controls

  Cause: the pale orange glow around the shop's search field was drawn on
  an invisible box around the field, and that box has square corners. So
  every tap on the search field drew a square pale-orange box around the
  rounded one.
  Fixed site-wide (shop and admin):
    - The glow now sits on the rounded field itself.
    - The focus ring shows only for keyboard users (":focus-visible").
      A tap or a click never draws an outline.
    - Plain links and buttons that have no rounded corners of their own
      (like "See all") get softly rounded corners while the keyboard ring
      shows, so the ring is never a hard square.
    - The blue/grey flash on tap was already off for the whole site
      (Batch 27); the tests now check that it stays off.
  Keyboard users still get a clear, rounded orange ring (accessibility).

PART 3 — "Just sold out" at checkout

  A cart doesn't hold stock (the normal rule: whoever orders first gets
  it). The database already refuses an order when stock is short. Now the
  second customer sees a calm message instead of an error:
      "Sorry, CeraVe Foaming Cleanser just sold out. We've updated your cart."
      "Only 1 left of CeraVe Foaming Cleanser — we've updated the quantity."
  - The cart corrects itself in one step: sold-out lines are removed,
    quantities are lowered to what's left, and totals update (a percent
    promo code also shrinks with the new subtotal, exactly as the database
    calculates it). Everything else stays, so the customer can carry on.
  - The check also runs when the cart page opens and when the order
    summary opens (just before the last step), so it is usually caught
    early.
  - The customer never sees the database's own wording. If an order is
    refused for stock but the live check finds nothing to fix, they see
    "Something in your cart just changed. Please check your cart and try
    again."
  - Admin New order: the existing "Low stock — continue anyway?" question
    stays (you sometimes oversell on purpose). The affected line now also
    shows "Sold out — none left in stock" or "Only N in stock" in red, and
    this stays after you press "Go back".

5. DESIGN DECISIONS
-------------------
  - Touch hold of 200 ms (the brief asked for about that). A finger that
    moves more than 8 px during the hold counts as a scroll, not a drag.
  - Mouse drag starts after 6 px, so a plain click (for example on the X)
    is never mistaken for a drag.
  - The lifted photo grows slightly (4%) and gets a soft shadow. It moves
    only with transform (smooth on mid-range phones). Users who turn off
    animations get no grow effect.
  - The "just sold out" message is neutral grey with a line icon, with no
    orange (orange stays for the one main action, Checkout). It has a
    close button and also disappears once an order is placed.
  - The photo editor tries the small copy before the full photo: faster on
    a slow connection, and the full photo is the fallback.


6. INDUSTRY-LEVEL SUGGESTIONS
-----------------------------
  1. Show "Only N left" on the product page and cards when stock is low
     (for example 3 or fewer). Why: it is honest urgency that lifts sales,
     and it warns buyers before checkout, so fewer "just sold out"
     surprises. Size: small.
  2. "Notify me when back in stock" for sold-out products. Why: it turns a
     lost sale into a returning customer. Size: medium (needs a table and
     a message).
  3. Move the brands and home-tiles drag lists to the same dnd-kit setup.
     Why: one way of dragging everywhere, with PC mouse and keyboard
     support. Size: small–medium.
