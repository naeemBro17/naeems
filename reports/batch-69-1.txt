------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT I DID
------------------------------------------------------------

Sun and moon. The Order Placed scene now has a sky. In light mode a soft
sun sits above the road with a gentle glow that slowly breathes. In dark
mode the sun is replaced by a crescent moon and five small twinkling stars.
They rise in with the scene on the first arrival; on a reload, or with
"reduce motion" on, they are simply there and nothing moves.

Sound. When the parcel lands in the van there is now a soft "ting", and as
the van drives off a short, quiet engine sound. Both sounds are made by the
phone itself in code (no audio files to download, so no extra data cost).
A small speaker button at the right of the "Order Placed" title turns the
sound on or off. The phone remembers that choice for next time. The sound
never plays on a reload or with "reduce motion". If the customer switches
it off halfway through the scene, the next sound does not play.

Important about iPhones: Apple only lets a web page make sound after the
person has tapped something on that page. Our sound plays on its own
shortly after the page opens, so on an iPhone it will usually stay silent
unless the customer has tapped the speaker button. Android phones play it.
Nothing breaks either way; the page just stays quiet.

Cleaner details below the scene (your approved mockup, used exactly):
- "Your order NM-1234 has been placed." is now one line with the order
  number in bold. The separate "Order no." chip is gone.
- The arrival days are now date chips inside the summary card:
  "Arriving between" above two chips like "FRI 27 Nov" and "SAT 28 Nov"
  (or "Arriving on" above one chip when it is a single day). Screen readers
  still hear the full sentence, e.g. "Arriving Fri, 27 to Sat, 28 Nov".
- For Cash on Delivery orders the "Payment" row is gone, because the total
  row already says "Pay on delivery". bKash, Nagad and wallet orders still
  show the Payment row.
- The note is now just "We will confirm your order soon."
- "Keep shopping" (soft blue, with a shop icon) now sits beside the orange
  "View my order" button, instead of being a small grey link at the bottom.
  "View my order" is still the only orange button on the page.
- Invoice and Need help? are unchanged.
At 390 x 844 (a typical phone) the whole page, down to Invoice and Need
help?, fits on one screen without scrolling.

Admin "Send note" button. On the admin order page, the "Send note" button
in the "Note to customer" card is now the plain outlined button instead of
orange. This leaves one orange button on that screen: the main action for
the order's status. The button works exactly as before (same label, greyed
out until you type, same spinner). It reads clearly in both light and dark
admin, so I did not need to change its colour or weight.

Tests: new unit tests for the date chips and the sound switch. The browser
tests were updated to the new wording and layout, and new checks were
added: the sound button starts on, a tap turns it off, and it stays off
after a reload. The sun shows in light mode and the moon and stars in dark
mode. Everything still animates only "transform" and "opacity". At
360 px "Keep shopping" and the two date chips each stay on one line, and
at 390 px the page fits without scrolling. The admin test now expects no
orange button in the note card.

------------------------------------------------------------
6. INDUSTRY-LEVEL SUGGESTIONS
------------------------------------------------------------

1. Order confirmation by SMS (medium). Many first-time buyers in Bangladesh
   trust an SMS with the order number more than a web page. It would also
   reduce "did my order go through?" WhatsApp messages.
2. "Add to home screen" prompt after the first order (small). This is the
   moment the customer is most pleased, and the app already works as an
   installable app (PWA). It brings repeat visits back with one tap.
3. The sound is quiet and switchable, but some shops leave sound off until
   the customer opts in (small). Watch whether customers turn it off; if
   many do, make it off by default.
